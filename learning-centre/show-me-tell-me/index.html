Show Me, Tell Me questions

All 21 official car vehicle safety questions, with explanations and flashcards.

You are asked two vehicle safety questions on your car test: a tell me question before you drive and a show me question while driving. A wrong answer to one or both is one driving fault. You fail if your driving becomes dangerous or potentially dangerous while answering the show me question.

Question wording and tell me answers are from the official DVSA list on GOV.UK (reproduced under the Open Government Licence v3.0, list last updated 4 December 2017). DVSA publishes no answers for the show me questions, so our notes on those are school guidance only. Controls differ between cars, so learn yours. Check the official list.

Tell me questions (14)

Tell me how you'd check that the brakes are working before starting a journey.
Official answer (DVSA): Brakes should not feel spongy or slack. Brakes should be tested as you set off. Vehicle should not pull to one side.

In plain English: Press the brake pedal: it should feel firm. Then, when you first move off slowly, brake gently and check the car stays straight.

Safety: Test the brakes at low speed in a safe, clear place.

Tell me where you'd find the information for the recommended tyre pressures for this car and how tyre pressures should be checked.
Official answer (DVSA): Manufacturer's guide, use a reliable pressure gauge, check and adjust pressures when tyres are cold, don't forget spare tyre, remember to refit valve caps.

In plain English: The car's handbook lists the right pressures (some cars also show them inside the door or fuel flap). Use a gauge, ideally when the tyres are cold.

Safety: Wrong pressures affect grip, braking and tyre wear.

Tell me how you make sure your head restraint is correctly adjusted so it provides the best protection in the event of a crash.
Official answer (DVSA): The head restraint should be adjusted so the rigid part of the head restraint is at least as high as the eye or top of the ears, and as close to the back of the head as is comfortable. Note: Some restraints might not be adjustable.

In plain English: The head restraint is there to limit whiplash. Set the top level with your eyes or the tops of your ears, and close to the back of your head.

Safety: Adjust it before you drive, not on the move.

Tell me how you'd check the tyres to ensure that they have sufficient tread depth and that their general condition is safe to use on the road.
Official answer (DVSA): No cuts and bulges, 1.6mm of tread depth across the central three-quarters of the breadth of the tyre, and around the entire outer circumference of the tyre.

In plain English: Look at each tyre for cuts and bulges. The legal minimum tread depth is 1.6mm across the central three-quarters of the tyre and all the way round.

Safety: Tread helps clear water, so worn tyres lengthen stopping distances in the wet.

Tell me how you'd check that the headlights and tail lights are working. You don't need to exit the vehicle.
Official answer (DVSA): Explain you'd operate the switch (turn on ignition if necessary), then walk round vehicle (as this is a 'tell me' question, you don't need to physically check the lights).

In plain English: Switch the lights on, then say you would walk round the car to check each light is working.

Safety: You only need to explain it. You do not need to get out.

Tell me how you'd know if there was a problem with your anti-lock braking system.
Official answer (DVSA): Warning light should illuminate if there is a fault with the anti-lock braking system.

In plain English: If there is a fault, an ABS warning light shows on the dashboard.

Safety: Do not ignore the light. Have the car checked.

Tell me how you'd check the direction indicators are working. You don't need to exit the vehicle.
Official answer (DVSA): Explain you'd operate the switch (turn on ignition if necessary), and then walk round vehicle (as this is a 'tell me' question, you don't need to physically check the lights).

In plain English: Switch the indicators on, then say you would walk round to check the front, side and rear lights flash.

Safety: You only need to explain it.

Tell me how you'd check the brake lights are working on this car.
Official answer (DVSA): Explain you'd operate the brake pedal, make use of reflections in windows or doors, or ask someone to help.

In plain English: Press the brake pedal and check the lights, using reflections in a window or door, or ask someone to look.

Safety: Brake lights warn drivers behind you.

Tell me how you'd check the power-assisted steering is working before starting a journey.
Official answer (DVSA): If the steering becomes heavy, the system may not be working properly. Before starting a journey, 2 simple checks can be made. Gentle pressure on the steering wheel, maintained while the engine is started, should result in a slight but noticeable movement as the system begins to operate. Alternatively turning the steering wheel just after moving off will give an immediate indicator that the power assistance is functioning.

In plain English: Power steering makes the wheel light. Hold gentle pressure on the wheel while starting the engine and feel it move slightly, or check how it feels as you move off.

Safety: Heavy steering is a warning sign.

Tell me how you'd switch on the rear fog light(s) and explain when you'd use it/them. You don't need to exit the vehicle.
Official answer (DVSA): Operate switch (turn on dipped headlights and ignition if necessary). Check warning light is on. Explain use.

In plain English: Rear fog lights are bright. Switch them on (dipped headlights first in many cars), check the dashboard light, and explain they are for seriously reduced visibility, such as thick fog.

Safety: Switch them off when visibility improves, as they can dazzle drivers behind.

Tell me how you switch your headlight from dipped to main beam and explain how you'd know the main beam is on.
Official answer (DVSA): Operate switch (with ignition or engine on if necessary), check with main beam warning light.

In plain English: Push or pull the light stalk. A blue light on the dashboard shows main beam is on.

Safety: Dip main beam for oncoming traffic and when following other vehicles.

Open the bonnet and tell me how you'd check that the engine has sufficient oil.
Official answer (DVSA): Identify dipstick/oil level indicator, describe check of oil level against the minimum and maximum markers.

In plain English: Find the dipstick, pull it out and wipe it, put it back, pull it out again and check the level sits between minimum and maximum.

Safety: You need to open the bonnet for this one. Do it with the engine off and cool.

Open the bonnet and tell me how you'd check that the engine has sufficient engine coolant.
Official answer (DVSA): Identify high and low level markings on header tank where fitted or radiator filler cap, and describe how to top up to correct level.

In plain English: Find the coolant tank and check the level between the high and low marks.

Safety: Never open a hot cooling system. Wait until the engine is cool.

Open the bonnet and tell me how you'd check that you have a safe level of hydraulic brake fluid.
Official answer (DVSA): Identify reservoir, check level against high and low markings.

In plain English: Find the brake fluid reservoir and check the fluid is between the high and low marks.

Safety: A falling level can point to a leak or worn pads. Get it checked.

Show me questions (7)

When it's safe to do so, can you show me how you wash and clean the rear windscreen?
What to do (school guidance, not an official DVSA answer): Find the rear washer/wiper control (it varies between cars) and use it. Check where it is before test day.

In plain English: Find the rear washer/wiper control (it varies between cars) and use it. Check where it is before test day.

Safety: Only do this when it is safe. The examiner will mark you down if your driving becomes dangerous while you answer.

When it's safe to do so, can you show me how you wash and clean the front windscreen?
What to do (school guidance, not an official DVSA answer): Use the wiper stalk to operate the washers and wipers. Find the setting for your car.

In plain English: Use the wiper stalk to operate the washers and wipers. Find the setting for your car.

Safety: Only do this when it is safe. The examiner will mark you down if your driving becomes dangerous while you answer.

When it's safe to do so, can you show me how you'd switch on your dipped headlights?
What to do (school guidance, not an official DVSA answer): Operate the light switch and point to the dashboard light that confirms it.

In plain English: Operate the light switch and point to the dashboard light that confirms it.

Safety: Only do this when it is safe. The examiner will mark you down if your driving becomes dangerous while you answer.

When it's safe to do so, can you show me how you'd set the rear demister?
What to do (school guidance, not an official DVSA answer): Find the heated rear window button and press it. Know which symbol it uses.

In plain English: Find the heated rear window button and press it. Know which symbol it uses.

Safety: Only do this when it is safe. The examiner will mark you down if your driving becomes dangerous while you answer.

When it's safe to do so, can you show me how you'd operate the horn?
What to do (school guidance, not an official DVSA answer): Press the horn button. Do it only when safe and when it will not startle other road users.

In plain English: Press the horn button. Do it only when safe and when it will not startle other road users.

Safety: Only do this when it is safe. The examiner will mark you down if your driving becomes dangerous while you answer.

When it's safe to do so, can you show me how you'd demist the front windscreen?
What to do (school guidance, not an official DVSA answer): Set the fan, temperature, air direction (screen) and air conditioning if needed. Controls differ by car.

In plain English: Set the fan, temperature, air direction (screen) and air conditioning if needed. Controls differ by car.

Safety: Only do this when it is safe. The examiner will mark you down if your driving becomes dangerous while you answer.

When it's safe to do so, can you show me how you'd open and close the side window?
What to do (school guidance, not an official DVSA answer): Use the window switch. Find which button is yours.

In plain English: Use the window switch. Find which button is yours.
