Driving dictionary

Plain-English definitions of driving terms.


16 terms shown

Biting point
The point where the clutch plates begin to connect and the car starts to move. You feel the engine note change slightly.
Blind spot
An area around the car you cannot see in your mirrors. Check it by looking over your shoulder before moving off or changing position.
Box junction
A junction marked with yellow criss-cross lines. Do not enter unless your exit is clear (you may wait to turn right if only oncoming traffic stops you).
Carriageway
The part of the road used by vehicles. A dual carriageway has a central reservation separating the two directions.
Clutch control
Using the clutch pedal smoothly to control the car's speed at very low speeds, for example when manoeuvring.
Dual carriageway
A road with a central reservation or barrier separating traffic travelling in opposite directions.
Emerging
Pulling out from a junction onto another road. Look carefully, then go only when it is safe.
Hazard
Anything that may require you to change speed or direction, such as a pedestrian stepping out or a parked car door opening.
Independent driving
A part of the practical test where you drive for a while without step-by-step directions, following signs or a sat nav.
Junction
A place where two or more roads meet. Examples include T-junctions, crossroads and roundabouts.
National speed limit
The maximum speed for the type of road and vehicle when no other limit is shown. For cars it is 60 mph on single carriageways and 70 mph on dual carriageways and motorways.
Observation
Looking around you, ahead, to the sides and in mirrors, to understand what is going on and plan ahead.
Roundabout
A junction where traffic moves around a central island. Give way to traffic from the right unless signs or markings say otherwise.
Serious fault
A driving fault that is potentially dangerous. It fails the test.
Stopping distance
The distance you travel while you react (thinking distance) plus the distance you travel once you brake (braking distance).
Undue hesitation
Waiting when it is safe to go, so you hold up other road users.
